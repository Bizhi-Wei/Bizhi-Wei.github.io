About This Blog

More than a resume — this is a place for honest recording and thoughtful sharing.

I am an undergraduate medical student interested in vascular surgery, surgical critical care, evidence-based medicine, MIMIC-IV database studies, molecular docking, and translational medicine.

Unlike a formal academic homepage, this site is designed to be approachable and reflective. Here, I want to write about how I started, what I struggled with, how I learned, and how each project shaped me.

In the future, I plan to build a separate, more rigorous academic CV homepage. But this site will remain my personal corner for research stories and growth.

My Research Journey

A few milestones I would like to remember.

Pediatric Appendectomy Meta-analysis

Early Research Experience

My early experience with evidence-based medicine taught me how systematic reviews and meta-analyses are not only about statistics, but also about patience, criticism, and academic rigor.

RAAA: EVAR versus Open Repair

Current Main Project

This project helped me think more deeply about clinical decision-making, hemodynamic selection bias, and the challenge of interpreting evidence in critical vascular conditions.

MIMIC-IV and Prediction Modeling

Database Research Exploration

Through database studies, I gradually learned how clinical questions can be transformed into structured, analyzable data problems, especially in surgical ICU settings.

Molecular Docking and MD

New Skill Expansion

This is where I began stepping from clinical thinking toward mechanistic exploration, trying to connect disease, targets, and candidate compounds in a more translational way.
